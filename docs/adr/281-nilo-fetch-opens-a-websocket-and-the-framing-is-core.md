# nilo_fetch opens a WebSocket, and the framing is nilo_core's

**Status:** accepted
**Topic:** [fetch](../design/fetch.md)
**Extends:** [ADR 061](./061-a-fitting-borrows-the-loop.md), the Client, its Call and the Exchange; [ADR 057](./057-percent-is-needed-by-two-layers.md), the files Core admits; [ADR 021](./021-a-websocket-is-a-handler-that-does-not-return.md), whose server now reads and writes the frame through Core. **Applies:** [ADR 017](./017-the-trade-budget-has-four-axes.md), [ADR 032](./032-a-guard-is-not-a-guard-until-it-has-been-seen-to-fail.md), [ADR 038](./038-a-module-sits-where-the-loop-puts-it.md), [ADR 046](./046-a-message-is-copied-once-and-framed-once.md), [ADR 056](./056-the-way-out-was-open-the-clock-was-not.md), [ADR 105](./105-a-route-can-say-how-long-it-has.md), [ADR 267](./267-a-call-can-go-through-a-proxy-and-trust-a-private-authority.md), [ADR 272](./272-a-call-names-the-socket-it-goes-over.md).
**Found by:** the todo entry "`nilo_fetch` has no WebSocket client", and reading what coder/websocket, tungstenite, undici, gorilla and awc each settled.

## Context

A service that consumes a feed (prices) or holds a chat platform's gateway open brought a WebSocket library of its own, and with it a second TLS configuration, a second idea of a deadline and a second set of limits, beside the `nilo_fetch` client that already had all three. The frame code was in `http/websocket.zig`, which `nilo_fetch` may not import (ADR 038), and a second copy of the framing is the thing to avoid: a masking loop that differs by a byte between the two ends is a bug that shows only against the other end.

What the others settled, and which of it this adopts:

- **A limit on a message, counted in decoded bytes.** coder/websocket 32 KiB, tungstenite 64 MiB, undici 128 MiB; gorilla counts frame bytes and has no default, which leaves a compressed message unbounded. Adopted, counted after decoding.
- **A ping answered without the caller.** tungstenite queues the pong, awc leaves it to the caller, who forgets. Adopted.
- **A close that waits a bounded time for the other side's.** coder/websocket, 5 s. Adopted.

## Decision

**The frame is `nilo_core.ws_frame`, and the server reads and writes it from there.** `core/ws_frame.zig` holds the opcodes and close codes, the header parse (`headerFrom`, `headerSize`) and write (`writeHeader`, `writeMaskedHeader`), the masking (`unmask`, `unmaskInto`, `maskInto`) with its SIMD tiers, `Frame.wellFormed(sender)` (the rules a header is held to: no reserved bit, masked from a client and unmasked from a server, the length in its shortest form, a control frame small and whole), the close payload (`closeIsWellFormed`, `reasonFits`, `closePayload`), the text rule (`validText`) and the handshake's `accept`. It imports `std` only, so `zig test core/core.zig` runs its table (RFC 6455 §5.2 as byte strings, masking against the byte-at-a-time loop at every length and key alignment). `http/websocket.zig` aliases them and keeps its public names (`Close`, `accept`, `max_header`, `headerFor`). It is the sixth file Core admits by the test of ADR 057: needed by two layers, one of which cannot import the other.

**`fetch.WebSocket` is an `Exchange` that was upgraded.** `ws.open(client, c, url, call)` is a GET asking for `Upgrade: websocket` begun through `Exchange.begin`, so it is every decision `nilo_fetch` has already made about a call and none of it written again: the gate, `timeout_ms` and the route's deadline narrowing it (ADR 105), the system's roots or the caller's bundle and TLS (ADR 267), `Call.unix_socket` (ADR 272, `ws://` over a socket works and `wss://` is `error.TlsOverSocket`), the request id and the trace. `ws://` and `wss://` are rewritten to `http://` and `https://` for std, which has no other idea of a scheme; `http(s)://` is accepted too. On a `101` whose `Upgrade`, `Connection` and `Sec-WebSocket-Accept` are right, `Exchange.upgraded()` hands over the connection (marked closing, so closing it destroys it and no idle HTTP connection ever carries WebSocket bytes), the open's clocks stop, and **the permit goes back**: `max_in_flight` counts calls in flight, and thirty-two sockets that live for hours would otherwise be the end of every other call the client makes. Anything else is `error.UpgradeRefused` with the status in `ws.status` (a 401 is a credential to refresh, a 429 a wait); a `101` that is not the answer to this handshake, an extension nobody offered or a subprotocol nobody offered is `error.BadHandshake`. A failed open leaves the socket as it was, so a reconnect loop opens the same variable again.

**The API.** `receive()` returns the next message (`.text` or `.binary`, `data` borrowed until the next call) or null when the conversation is over, whether by a close frame or by the connection stopping (`closedCleanly()`, `closeCode()` and `closeReason()` say which). `send`, `sendText`, `sendBinary`, `sendJson(value)` and `ping` write one frame. `close(code, reason)` returns `.acknowledged`, `.timed_out` or `.dropped`. `deinit()` never waits. It follows the server's `Socket` where the cases are the same (a message is borrowed, `receive` ends in null rather than an error) and differs where they are not (a `send` on a closed socket is `error.Closed`: the server's silence is for a loop that cannot prevent the race, and a client that wrote to a feed which was already gone should be told).

**Every frame the client sends is masked with four fresh bytes from `std.Io`** (RFC 6455 §5.3). The mask is applied while the payload is copied into the connection's write buffer, in one pass and with no scratch (the server's finding of ADR 046 turned round), and the caller's slice is never written to. `io.random` rather than `randomSecure`: the key is not a secret (its job is to stop the bytes being chosen by a page), and a syscall for each frame would cost a publisher more than the frame.

**The limit is `Call.max_message`, 1 MiB by default, counted in decoded bytes across all the pieces of a message.** A frame announcing more than is left of it is refused on its header, before a byte of it is read, and the socket is closed with 1009 (`error.MessageTooBig`). The default is set against the cases: a price feed's message is hundreds of bytes and a chat platform's event a few kilobytes, so for both the ceiling is never near; the case that sets it is the gateway whose first message is large, and 1 MiB holds a bot's `READY` on all but the largest platforms, which raise the field. The other pull is the peer that goes wrong, and what that costs is one buffer per socket, which is why it is nearer coder/websocket's 32 KiB than undici's 128 MiB. The buffer is paid at the size of the largest message that arrived, not in advance, and a message that arrived whole in the read buffer (every one a feed sends, and nearly every one under 8 KiB) takes no buffer and no copy at all: a server never masks, so there is nothing to undo.

**A ping is answered inside `receive`**, with its payload, masked, and the caller sees nothing. A pong is swallowed. A close is echoed and ends `receive`. Text that is not UTF-8 is 1007 (`error.InvalidPayload`), a header that breaks the rules 1002 (`error.ProtocolError`), each sent before the error is returned.

**`close` waits at most `Call.close_timeout_ms` (5 s)** for the other side's close frame, on one clock that covers the write of its own, and reads and drops whatever else arrives meanwhile so a peer that keeps sending cannot hold it open. A peer that never answers is `.timed_out` and the connection is destroyed anyway.

**Silence is `Call.idle_ms`, off by default.** A quiet socket is working (a gateway with nobody typing), so nothing ends it unasked; a feed that must not go quiet sets it above the server's heartbeat and `receive` is `error.Stalled` after that long with nothing at all arriving, a ping included. It is `Exchange`'s `stall_ms` clock, stamped at frame boundaries and armed per `receive`, so under an Engine it is the same timer re-armed per frame and with none it is a task of the `Io` per call to `receive`. The open's `timeout_ms` bounds the open and nothing after it.

**One fiber at a time.** `receive` writes the pongs, so a second fiber in `send` while the first is in `receive` would interleave two frames in one buffer. The server's `Socket` is the same; a feed read in one place and written to from another is two sockets or a queue between them.

**No permessage-deflate.** The server does not have it either (it negotiates no extension and refuses a frame with a reserved bit), the client offers none and refuses an answer that names one, and with it left out the decoded-byte limit and the frame-byte limit agree. It is an extension that changes the count above and the buffer below, and it belongs in one change that does both ends.

**Cost, on the four axes.** *Allocations per request*: none on the server's path; the request-path budget test holds. A client message that arrived whole allocates nothing (0 allocations and 0 bytes a round trip, `bench/release/fetch_ws.zig`); the open spends the arena for the rewritten URL and the header lines and a join of the subprotocols. *Memory per idle connection*: the server is unchanged, 5,186 and 5,190 bytes before and after for a socket in a Room at 2,000 sockets, and `park-check` reads one page in all four builds. A `WebSocket` is 1,200 bytes on the frame of the fiber that holds it (an `Exchange` is 992 of them), which is the handler's stack while it waits in `receive` (ADR 062); the figure for a handler parked in `receive` has not been measured. *Throughput*: the server's frame path is the same code, **683.7 and 683.9 user instructions a 40-byte echo and 8,599.0 and 8,596.8 a 16 KiB one, before and after** (a counter read through `perf_event_open`, difference over difference), and the server's CPU a message agrees inside its spread. The client round trip is 1,920 instructions with the upstream's half included against 9,745 for a GET on a kept-alive connection. *Binary*: a program that dials out and opens no socket is **the same size to the byte** (946,688 stripped `ReleaseFast`, before and after); one that opens a socket is 9,472 bytes larger than the GET program, the upstream stub and the client together (`bench/result/fetch.md`, `bench/result/http.md`).

## What was rejected

**A second copy of the frame code in `nilo_fetch`.** The cheapest change, and a masking loop that has to be kept equal by hand to the one it talks to. ADR 057's test is exactly this case.

**Moving the whole of `http/websocket.zig`, or a `Socket` in Core.** The handshake, `Room` and the park machinery need the Engine's `Waker`; what the client shares is the bytes, and Core stops at the bytes.

**A `Target.websocket(c, path, args, call)`.** It would give a service its base URL and credential once. It composes (a target's `base` is the URL's prefix and its standing headers go in `Call.headers`) and nothing here needs it first; it waits for a service that asks (`docs/todo.md`).

**The message in the Scope's arena, as a `Str`.** The shape of every other `nilo_fetch` answer, and wrong for a call that does not end: a feed read for hours would grow the request's arena by every message. The loan until the next `receive` is the server's `Message` (ADR 021), the same trade for the same reason.

**A permit held for the life of the socket.** Counts a socket as a call in flight and lets thirty-two of them starve the client; the gate is for the cost of a connection being made, which is over at the `101`.

**A deadline on the socket as a whole.** A socket is allowed to live as long as it likes; the clocks that exist are the open's and the silence's, and the close's own.

**`randomSecure` for the mask.** A syscall a frame, for a key that is not a secret.

**Answering a close frame's reason to the caller as an error.** A close is the end of a conversation, which `receive` already says with null; the code and reason are there to read (`closeCode`, `closeReason`) for the caller that cares.

## Consequences

`nilo_core` gains `ws_frame`, and `http/websocket.zig` loses about 400 lines and keeps every public name. Nothing a user wrote changes. `fetch.Exchange` gains `upgraded`, `releasePermit`, `startClock`, `watchSilence`, `stopClocks` and `clockFired`, and makes `bounded`, `blame` and `mark` public, because the WebSocket keeps an Exchange of its own to bound its reads; they are the seams a second long-lived call would use. The test that earned the engine half is the one that saw `Stalled` come back as `ReadFailed`: std collapses a read the clock cancelled into an ended stream or `ReadFailed`, and `receive` treated an ended stream as the ordinary end until `fetch/deadline.zig` ran it under the Engine's timer (ADR 032); `Exchange.clockFired` is the question that now comes first.
